Life Is a Game, and Nobody Gave You the Rules. Here Are 5.

One of my favourite spiritual teachers said this in the first chapter of her book:

“Most people consider life a battle, but it is not a battle, it is a game. “—Florence Scovel Shinn (The Game of Life and How to Play It)

Life is a game; it has its own rules, and it is played on the field of the mind. Now, I have to elaborate on that: what is the field of the mind? The field of the mind is the field of thoughts and emotions. Your thoughts and emotions are the causes of your life experiences. To “be” or “get” what you want, you must first know that it has to be impressed on your mind; your desires must be emotionally impressed on your mind. And how is this done? It is done through your Beliefs.

Beliefs are accepted ideas that determine how you think and feel about yourself, other people and the world around you. If you can give yourself some time to pay attention to your beliefs or the ideas you have accepted, you will learn that your beliefs determine the things you think about and how you feel.

So, to effectively play the game of life, you must intentionally use your own mind by being intentional with your beliefs, thoughts and emotions in relation to what you want to achieve, get or experience.

The following are the 5 rules you can use to play the game of life in the field of your own creative mind:

“Life is a game and, like all games, it has its aims and its rules.”—Neville Goddard (Seedtime and Harvest)

Rule 1

In the game of life, there is only the goal, and what is the goal? To get that which you desire. Therefore, you must have the desire or know what you want.

“There is no opponent in the game of life; there is only the goal.”—Neville Goddard (Seedtime and Harvest)

By knowing what you want to get, achieve, experience or be, you give your mind a goal to work towards. A goal and a desire are one and the same thing, and there is no way you can effectively play the game of life without having a goal. There is a risk of having no goal, and that is an uncontrollable or chaotic mind (chaotic thoughts and emotions). There is profound gain in having a goal or following your desires, and that is focused attention toward attaining what you desire.

  • Always have a goal or go for the things you truly desire.

Rule 2

Believe you are worthy of what you desire and believe that you have it already. By doing this, you will be changing your identity or how you see yourself. As you change your beliefs or identity, you will change the working patterns of your mind. The thing that will get you what you want is the belief that you are worthy of it; that is to say, you get the things you want because you are fully confident that you deserve them.

To get what you want, you must believe yourself to be someone who deserves it. Believe or assume that you already have you desire.

“For example; if you assume that you have a wonderful business, you will notice how in your imagination, your attention is focused on incident after incident relating to that assumption. Friends congratulate you, tell you how lucky you are. Others are envious and critical. From there, your attention goes to larger offices, bigger bank balances, and many other similarly related events.”—Neville Goddard (The Power of Awareness)

Your beliefs or assumptions apply in everything: material success, relationships and so on.

  • Believe you are worthy of what you desire and believe that you have it already.

Rule 3

Pay Attention to your mental conversations.

"Thinking follows the tracks laid down in one's own inner conversations."—Neville Goddard (Seedtime and Harvest)

Thinking is just like any other physical activity. You can do it effectively or carelessly; if you can (and as you should), you must think effectively; you must use your thoughts or mental conversations positively.

Why is it so important to think positively?

Because it will make you feel good about yourself, other people and the world around you. This will keep your confidence intact, and confidence that is built innately will always, without fail, lead to better intuition and more creativity. It does not matter whether you are a leader, entrepreneur, friend, lover or colleague; this rule still applies.

Be aware of the mental conversations you have with other people or the ones you have about yourself, because your mental conversations determine the life experiences you will encounter in your reality.

“Most of us are totally unaware of the mental activity which goes on within us. But to play the game of life successfully, we must become aware of our every mental activity, for this activity, in the form of inner conversations, is the cause of the outer phenomena of our life.”—Neville Goddard (Seedtime and Harvest)

Whenever you catch yourself arguing, critiquing, or having tensions with other people or yourself, change that! Change it to something positive. If you catch yourself mentally arguing with someone, hear that person saying wonderful things about you, or say wonderful things about them. This will keep your mental conversations positive and help you feel better about yourself, other people and the world around you.

  • Have positive mental conversations.

Rule 4

Immerse yourself in the emotions you would feel if you had already achieved your desire.

“What would you think and say and do were you already the one you want to be? Begin to think and say and do this inwardly.”—Neville Goddard (Seedtime and Harvest)

The above quote is so profound, and I would like to add that you must also feel the emotions you would feel if you had your desire right now. Would you feel peaceful, calm, serene, grateful, secure, confident, ecstatic…? Start to immerse yourself in the emotions you would feel.

“Moods are not only the result of the conditions of our life; they are also the causes of those conditions.”—Neville Goddard (The Law and the Promise)

Emotions, just like beliefs and thoughts, are the causes of your reality. Your emotions determine the things you will achieve.

“We say that we are happy because we have achieved our goal; we do not realize that the process works equally well in the reverse direction—that we shall achieve our goal because we have assumed the happy feeling of the wish fulfilled.”—Neville Goddard (The Law and the Promise)

  • Immerse yourself in the emotions you would feel if you had already achieved your desire.

Rule 5

Pay attention to your intuition and the ideas you have as you go about your days. Act on the intuitive ideas that you have or write them down if you cannot act on them. Good and transformative ideas always come into our minds when we least expect them. Speaking of the patterns of the mind, one of the greatest spiritual teachers, Florence Scovel Shinn, said,

“There is a perfect picture of this in the superconscious mind. It usually flashes across the conscious as an unattainable ideal -"something too good to be true."

The perfect picture is the intuitive and creative ideas you will have when you entertain positive beliefs, good thoughts, and emotions.

As long as you are the person who already has what you desire, it will be so much easier to have thoughts, ideas, and intuition of that identity. Therefore, “be” and physical action will take care of itself.

“It is impossible to do anything. You must be in order to do.”—Neville Goddard (The Power of Awareness)

  • To take the right action, pay attention to your intuition and the ideas you have as you go about your days.

Conclusion: The 5 rules that you can use to effectively play the game of life:

  1. Always have a goal or go for the things you truly desire.
  2. Believe you are worthy of what you desire and believe that you have it already.
  3. Have positive mental conversations.
  4. Immerse yourself in the emotions you would feel if you had already achieved your desire
  5. To take the right action, pay attention to your intuition and the ideas you have as you go about your days.

Attention Is the Hidden Power Behind All Creation

Introduction

If there is one thing that has an impact on getting you the outcome you want is controlled and intentional attention.

Attention is a creative tool, and it is normally taken very lightly.

Attention is the messenger that brings you whatever you dearly desire.

Without controlled and intentional use of attention, you will get the things you do not want, and this is what most people do.

Here is how you can effectively control and use your attention intentionally:

  1. Know what you want.
  2. Focus on the within, not the without: How often do you ensure that your attention (Thoughts and Emotions) is aligned with what you want? You should focus more on ensuring that you have thoughts that reflect the fulfillment of your desire.

“The‌ ‌great‌ ‌secret‌ ‌of‌ ‌success‌ ‌is‌ ‌to‌ ‌focus‌ ‌the‌ ‌attention‌ ‌on‌ ‌the‌‌ feeling‌ ‌of‌ ‌the‌ ‌wish‌ ‌fulfilled‌ ‌without‌ permitting‌ ‌any‌ ‌distraction.”—Neville Goddard (The Power of Awareness)

Focus your attention on already having what you now desire.

Your mental conversations determine your physical reality or what you will experience in life. Your emotions, or how you feel during the course of your days, determine what you will experience. If you already had your desire, you would feel secure, peaceful, grateful, etc. Therefore, infuse yourself with those emotions that reflect that you already have what you desire; feel those emotions now!

Think the thoughts you would think after getting what you want now! Your thoughts and emotions should be of the version of you that already has what you now desire to have or be. You want a successful business? Think and feel as if your business is already successful.

Have those thoughts and emotions during the course of the day. At first, your conscious mind will reject them, but persist in assuming that what you want, you already have it now. As long as you persist, what you assume will be a reality; you will experience or have it.

“Assume‌ ‌you‌ ‌are‌ ‌that‌ ‌which‌ ‌you‌ ‌wish‌ ‌to‌ ‌be;‌ ‌walk‌ ‌as‌ ‌though‌ ‌you‌ ‌were‌ ‌it;‌ ‌and‌‌ as‌ ‌you‌ ‌remain‌ ‌faithful‌ to‌ ‌your‌ ‌assumption‌—it‌ ‌will‌ ‌harden‌ ‌into‌ ‌fact.‌”—Neville Goddard (Five Lessons: A Masterclass)

  1. Practice Relaxation daily: Relaxation is the most essential practice you have to include in your life. Most people are stressed and tense because they never relax. By being tense, you get to be in a state of mental strain, and the only thing that would stem from this is making decisions that would not be of good value. By relaxing, you will have mental clarity, and you will also be more intuitive. Relax; you can do this by meditating or simply doing nothing for some minutes or for an hour, and as you are relaxed, pay attention to your thoughts; you might get fruitful ideas whilst you are relaxed. This is because when we are not tense—when we are relaxed—our brain waves get into states of Alpha and Theta; these are brain waves of the subconscious mind, which is the creative mind within every person on earth.

“The‌ ‌mechanism‌ ‌of‌ ‌creation‌ ‌is‌ ‌hidden‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌very‌ ‌depth‌ ‌of‌ ‌the‌‌ subconscious,‌ ‌the‌ ‌female‌ ‌aspect‌ ‌or‌ womb‌ ‌of‌ ‌creation.‌”—Neville Goddard (Feeling is the Secret)

As ideas come from this creative mind, note them down and act on them if you can. This is one of my favourite practices to do to get ideas on what to write about or to do any creative work. Practice relaxation.

  1. Guard your attention: If there is one other great practice you should adopt, it is guarding your own attention like it is a growing infant. Your attention is your creative tool; therefore, protect it at all costs. For me, I have some rules that I follow to help guard my attention, and these are:
  • I do not watch the news.
  • I spend less time on social media.
  • I spend some time alone.
  • I have my own beliefs which are aligned with what I want and so on.

I do not know which one of the above attention-guarding tools you will adopt, but I would recommend you adopt one or some of them or even come up with your own.

We live in a world where we scatter our creative force—attention—into issues that we should not be scattering our forces on in the first place. Your attention is more valuable than anything else because it is what brings you experiences.

For instance, you cannot say you want to be in a happy romantic relationship and spend all your day watching or consuming content of relationship failures, drama, and heartbreaks. And of course, such content can be packaged as “entertainment,” and my question to you would be this: are your desires a form of entertainment or are they worth a better investment of your attention?

SUMMARY

  1. Know what you want.
  2. Focus on the within, not the without.
  3. Practice relaxation daily.
  4. Guard your attention.

“To‌ ‌the‌ ‌unenlightened‌ ‌man‌ ‌this‌ ‌will‌ ‌seem‌ ‌to‌ ‌be‌ ‌all‌ ‌fantasy,‌ ‌yet‌ ‌all‌ ‌progress‌‌ comes‌ ‌from‌ ‌those‌ who‌ ‌do‌ ‌not‌ ‌take‌ ‌the‌ ‌accepted‌ ‌view,‌ ‌nor‌ ‌accept‌ ‌the‌ ‌world‌‌ as‌ ‌it‌ ‌is.‌”—Neville Goddard (The Power of Awareness)

Why Your Ego Won’t Let You Adapt (And How to Fix It)

Introduction

To adapt is to attain knowledge that helps one evolve or progressively become successful and fulfilled. But the attainment of knowledge is not the problem; the problem is its Enemy.

I use Adaptability and progressive success and fulfillment interchangeably in this writing.

We live in an Information age; therefore, we cannot say people fail to succeed or progressively succeed because they lack knowledge or information. Attaining knowledge and practicing it is easy as long as we know where to find it and how to apply it, and of course, dealing with the Enemy of Adapting is vital as well—it is more vital than acquiring and applying knowledge.

To understand this a bit further, I would like to classify people into two different groups: one is an open-minded group, and the other is a fixed-minded group.

People who can Adapt

“He was the greatest conqueror the world ever knew because he was more open to learning than any other conqueror has ever been.”—Ryan Holiday (Ego is the Enemy)

People who can adapt are open-minded. By being open-minded, it is much easier to acquire and apply new knowledge/habits that could lead to the attainment of what they want. Being open-minded is the key to creativity and confidence—and creativity and confidence are the key to making progress in one’s life.

People who can adapt are always students; they never stop learning and looking for the best information or skill that can be used in whatever they want to accomplish.

As I was reading Ryan Holiday’s book, Ego Is the Enemy, specifically Part II ("Success"), chapter "Always Stay a Student." I read about an interesting story of The Legend of Genghis Khan who led the Mongols. During his reign, he adopted skills that were helpful to his empire.

“Under Genghis Khan’s direction, the Mongols were as ruthless about stealing and absorbing the best of each culture they encountered as they were about conquest itself.”—Ryan Holiday (Ego is the Enemy)

Genghis Khan’s empire was about conquering and learning from their enemies—it was not just about conquering the enemy.

Therefore, as you go through life, your biggest goal has to be learning and acquiring useful knowledge for the betterment of your life. And some of this knowledge will be foreign or new, that does not matter.

The important question you should ask yourself is this: Is the information valuable to my life? I have lost potential clients that could not work with me because I suggested they meditate or visualize; such practices were foreign or new to them, therefore, they could not adopt them into their lives.

At the end of this Article, I will share some practices you can do. The question you should ask is: Will this work for me? And the best answer to that is to simply try it out and see it for yourself.

I like to remind myself that every person has a divine being within them, and this being can do wonders if they make use of it. Hence why I like this Bible verse:

“Examine yourselves as to whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not know yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you?—unless indeed you are disqualified. But I trust that you will know that we are not disqualified.”—2 Corinthians 13:5–6

People who do not Adapt

Lack of adaptation (acquiring new knowledge/habits) leads to stagnant or slow growth. It is the acquisition of knowledge that we have to understand and practice. But the acquisition of knowledge is not the problem; therefore, let’s first deal with the Enemy.

After dealing with the Enemy of Adapting, it will be much easier to acquire knowledge/habits and practice them to the best of our abilities.

The Enemy of Adaptability and how to deal with it for good

What is the Enemy of Adaptability?

The Enemy of progressive success and fulfillment is Pathological Tribalism: it is a situation in which one is extremely loyal to his or her social group. This form of loyalty can be done consciously or unconsciously, and it determines the information one can absorb or reject, and in most cases, the information that is foreign or new will be rejected if it does not align with the beliefs held by the social group. **

You belong to different social groups: family, friends, religious community, and so on. All of these social groups determine the information you are to acquire in life, and in most cases, these social groups hinder the effectiveness of your success and fulfillment—if you do not ask the “right” questions and know where to find the “right” answers.

But, Pathological Tribalism is one side of the coin; the other side of it or the other enemy is: the Unknown Enemy within ourselves normally called, Denial. Both problems will be solved in this Article.

As I was reading a book called Facing the Dragon by Robert Moore, I came across this term: Pathological Tribalism. I wondered what it meant, and I went to Google to look it up; an incredible definition and its signs popped up on my screen. Some of the signs of Pathological Tribalism are as follows: Us vs Them; We are right, they are wrong; We are good, they are evil, etc.

It does not stop here; the effects of Pathological Tribalism are as follows: lack of critical thinking, fear of being exiled or treated as an outcast by your social group, loyalty without questioning beliefs, etc. Asking questions and reasons why some practices are done can put you in trouble; it is much safer not to ask questions or keep most things to yourself—and it is safe—but it is more adventurous not to live a “safe life”.

This writing is about being adventurous. I once heard my favourite speaker, Jim Rohn, saying something in the lines of “If you want to be safe, then hide in a corner, and we will feed you, but what a way to live?” To be safe rather than adventurous.

The Art of Adaptation: acquiring progressive knowledge

“And if the truth of all things always existed in the soul, then the soul is immortal. Wherefore be of good cheer, and try to recollect what you do not know, or rather what you do not remember.”— Meno, Plato (spoken by Socrates)

I like the idea that everything already exists within us and we can use our inner power and abilities to cultivate the feeling of already having what we want. When this feeling of already having what we want is cultivated and persisted in long enough, it becomes a reality.

To acquire progressive knowledge, we have to first deal with these two problems: Pathological Tribalism and Denial. Though they seem to be two different problems, they are two sides of the same coin.

How to deal with the problem of Pathological Tribalism?

  1. Change or question your beliefs. Find yourself time to question your beliefs about yourself, people, and the world around you. The thing about your beliefs is that they are the blueprint of our lives; our beliefs determine the actions we are to take and our life experiences. Ask yourself these questions: What are the beliefs held by my social group that I hold dearly but are against my desires or goals? For instance, if people around believe it is hard to get a job or jobs are limited, but you have the desire to get a job, then it would be sound to change the belief that jobs are limited and believe that jobs are unlimited. This sounds simple, but I guarantee you one thing: the truth is simple. Use your own Will and hold beliefs that align with your desire or what you want.
  2. Be comfortable with being different. One of the biggest enemies of progressive success and fulfilment is the fear of being different. Unless you want to be like people of your social group, you need to be different from them; this is not betraying them, it is simply choosing yourself in a world that is comfortable with self-neglect. And as you are comfortable with being different, you might inspire some people of your social group.

How to deal with Denial?

“The stability we cannot find in the world we must create within our own persons.”—Nathaniel Branden (THE SIX PILLARS OF SELF-ESTEEM)

“But the anointing which you have received from Him abides in you, and you do not need that anyone teach you; but as the same anointing teaches you concerning all things, and is true, and is not a lie, and just as it has taught you, you will abide in Him.”—1 John 2: 27

By changing ourselves, our outside world will also change, and the biggest secret is changing ourselves. Within every individual is a gem of creativity and great knowledge; it is best to make use of this inner part of us. There is a part in every individual that needs attention, and if it is not given the attention it seeks, it acts against what we want. What I have just shared is based on this book The Collected Works of C. G. Jung, Volume 8 (The Structure and Dynamics of the Psyche), edited and translated by Gerhard Adler and R. F. C. Hull, specifically the chapter "A Review of the Complex Theory."

This part of us comprises our truest desires and past experiences, especially the negative ones. These inner feelings are not bad nor negative; they are simply experiences that have to be understood, addressed, or given attention. This part of the writing gives a simple instruction on how that can be done.

  1. Cultivate Self-Awareness: Pay Attention to your Beliefs, Thoughts, and Emotions. Avoid denying how you feel. Simply be aware of the inner desires that you have within you; give those deep inner feelings attention.

Conclusion

Adapt. As you adapt, you will always evolve. Knowledge is always available to be practiced but first, deal with Pathological Tribalism and Denial.

To deal with Pathological Tribalism or Social groups: come up with your own Beliefs that favour the goals you want to achieve.

To deal with Denial: pay attention to the inner feelings and voices within yourself; just be aware of them—awareness is similar to giving the inner you attention; that is what the inner you wants: attention.
